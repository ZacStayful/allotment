Notes

The fenced neighbour
  • Left boundary faces ~340°; shadows from this plot run almost exactly that way
  • Shade layer spill readout measures live how far over the fence structures reach
  • Midsummer noon: ~0 m spill. Equinox noon: ~0.4 m. Midwinter: 7 m+ (dormant ground)
  • The culprit is a 2 m bean row starting 600 mm off the fence — hence the 1.5 m low-crop strip
  • First 1.5 m inside the fence is low-crop only: onions, carrots, lettuce, phacelia
  • Beans, staked tomatoes and sweetcorn start 1.5 m from the fence minimum
  • Do not use the fence as a trellis without checking whose it is and the association rules
The bramble edge
  • Two foot of bramble is an advancing edge — canes tip-root wherever they touch soil
  • Cut the face back hard every winter, keep the 700 mm margin open
  • Expect dry, root-filled soil within ~2 m of the edge
  • Thorny prunings do not go in the compost bays — burn or separate slow heap
  • Upside: the treeline shelters from cold spring easterlies; slower gentler thaw than an exposed plot
Watering
  • 40 m hose leaves ~10 m usable on the plot (30 m to the standpipe)
  • Far corners of woodland end sit outside the reach — see the water layer
  • Use 15 mm (5/8") bore, not 12.5 mm — better pressure over 30 m on a shared standpipe
  • Better habit: use the hose to fill butts, water from stored water at the beds
  • Gutter the tunnel not the shed: 8 m² tunnel roof ≈ 200 L per 25 mm rain; shed gives ~55 L
  • Tap off November–March: drain and lift hose each autumn, go into winter with butts full
  • Check association rules on hosepipes before buying
Build order
  1. Cut the bramble face back and mark the 700 mm margin — gets harder every month you leave it
  2. Peg out both side buffers at 600 mm — boundaries right on day one
  3. Woodchip the upper cross path — all traffic to shed, bays and tunnel crosses it
  4. Beds 1 and 2 first (nearest the gate): turves inverted, cardboard, subsoil, 10–12 cm bought compost
  5. Compost bays next — weeds and turf stop needing to be barrowed off-site
  6. Written committee consent, then shed (1.83 × 1.22 m), then polytunnel (4.0 × 2.0 m)
  7. Butts and tunnel gutter before first full summer — stored water is the thing you regret not having
  8. Phacelia into the woodland strip as soon as ground is clear
Permanent companions
  • Phacelia — woodland strip. Dig in green before it sets seed
  • Chives — along the spine path edge beside the root beds. Perennial, plant once
  • Borage — one corner of the nursery area. Self-seeds; give it one spot and thin annually
  • French marigolds — path edges throughout. Self-seeds; let some go to seed in September

Tunnel crops are fixed and do not rotate: tomatoes all season, basil between every tomato plant, French marigolds in pots at the tunnel ends so they stay moveable.
